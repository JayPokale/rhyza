Rhyza follows the <LinkOut href='https://openpayments.dev'>Open Payments</LinkOut> standard to enable third-party clients to securely retrieve account information and authorize payments from your customers' accounts with their consent. The standard describes a uniform way to create and manage grants and resources for <LinkOut href='https://openpayments.dev/concepts/resources/#resource-types'>incoming payments, quotes, and outgoing payments</LinkOut>.

## Rhyza's Open Payments services

Rhyza provides Open Payments functionality through standalone services that expose the necessary APIs for clients to securely retrieve account information and initiate payments.

### Open Payments Resource Server

The Open Payments Resource Server is a standalone service that exposes the endpoints defined in the <LinkOut href="https://openpayments.dev/apis/resource-server/">Open Payments specification</LinkOut>. It acts as a stateless translation layer that validates incoming requests and communicates with internal system modules (such as Payments and PAL) to manage resources like incoming payments, quotes, and outgoing payments.

### Open Payments Auth Server

The Open Payments Auth Server is a standalone service that implements the Open Payments authorization server specification. It is responsible for delegating authorization (via grants) to clients, resolving public keys for authentication, and managing access tokens. Open Payments leverages the Grant Negotiation and Authorization Protocol (GNAP) for delegating authorization.

[Learn more about the system modules](/overview/overview#system-architecture)

## Example use cases

### Retrieving account information

Your customer uses a third-party app that allows them to create budgets and monitor their spending. To get the information it needs, the app uses the Open Payments APIs to request your customer's transaction history.

### eCommerce payments

Your customer begins a purchase from an online merchant. Since the merchant has implemented Open Payments, your customer can enter their wallet address into the merchant's checkout form instead of their credit card details. The merchant's server uses the Open Payments APIs to communicate with you to set up the payment and get your customer's consent for the purchase.

### Peer-to-peer payments (for example, remittances)

Your customer uses a third-party remittance app to send money from the US to their family in Mexico. They want their father to receive an exact amount in Mexican pesos, regardless of how much it will cost in USD. Since the app developer has implemented Open Payments, your customer can enter the wallet address for themselves and their father instead of entering bank account details. The app uses the Open Payments APIs to communicate with you to set up the payment and get your customer's consent for the payment.
